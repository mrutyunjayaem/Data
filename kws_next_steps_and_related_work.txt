Related work to position against

The spotter you built (reproduction context)

The DFSMN, CTC and CDC design follows a published streaming approach. It proposes a streaming decoding algorithm that detects the keyword start at any position, and uses cross-layer discrimination consistency to separate true keywords from false alarm samples.
arxiv
A follow-up extends CDC to multiple decoding heads. It combines a CTC-Transducer system with multi-head frame-asynchronous decoding and reports a 47–63% speed-up over frame-synchronous baselines.
arxiv
A noise-robust variant uses the same backbone. NTC-KWS builds on a 6-layer DFSMN with 512 hidden and 320 projection sizes, and reports strong results at low SNR on clean and noisy Hey Snips.
arxiv
A newer two-stage line reports 99.13% recall at one false alarm per hour on Hey-Snips in a zero-shot setting. This is a useful comparison point for your recall numbers.
arxiv

How KWS false alarms are usually treated (what your work contrasts with)

The standard view is per-threshold, not temporal. A survey describes streaming mode, where a keyword is detected each time the score exceeds a tuned threshold, and the same keyword occurrence can trigger repeatedly across consecutive segments. This relates directly to your 2 s event merging.
arxiv
Older confidence work focuses on scoring each hypothesis, not on timing. For example, keyword hypothesis scores are treated as a confidence estimation problem.
cam

Reliability monitoring without ground truth (closest to your “watch only” layer)

Performance monitoring predicts ASR quality from system behavior without ground truth, using entropy, the M-measure and an RNN predictor, with an average prediction error of 8.8%. Your entropy and blank features come from this family.
arxiv
A more recent label-free line trains a regression model on features to predict WER and CER without labels.
arxiv

HMM and HSMM for health states and failure prediction (source of your RQ2 framing)

Prognostics work uses a similar structure. One model has healthy, warning and failure states, with only failure observable and sojourn times modelled explicitly, and a higher-order HSMM framework estimates hidden health and remaining useful life.
peeref
nsf
A reliability review notes that choosing the number of HMM states and predicting failures are not well covered in the literature. This supports your point about state-count selection.
ugr

How your work fits

Strengths: you evaluate temporal structure of KWS false alarms with proper nulls, and test a state-based early-warning idea from the reliability-engineering tradition. A clear negative result is a defensible contribution.
Caveat: the engineering-reliability literature assumes a degradation path (healthy → warning → failure). Your data show states lasting about 1 s, so that framing doesn’t transfer at this resolution, and you should say so directly.
Missing piece: I didn’t find a KWS paper that measures temporal clustering of false alarms against noise and speaker nulls. If that holds after a more thorough search, it is your strongest novelty claim.
